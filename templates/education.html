Financial Education Learning Modules

Foundational financial literacy guides tailored for college students, explaining practical budgeting, interest dynamics, and risk management in plain language.

1. The 50 / 30 / 20 Budgeting Rule
A balanced framework for monthly cash flow

A simple guideline for distributing monthly pocket money, stipend, or part-time income:

50% Needs: Essential living expenses such as mess/food, hostel rent, transport, and tuition books.
30% Wants: Lifestyle expenses like weekend movies, dining out, streaming subscriptions, and hobbies.
20% Savings & Debt: Uncompromised savings toward emergency fund reserves and future investments.
Student Pro-Tip: Track weekly cash spending in a simple notebook or mobile app before planning investments.
2. Inflation & Purchasing Power
Why money in cash loses value over time

Inflation is the gradual rise in prices of goods and services over time. If the annual inflation rate is 6%:

  • An item costing ₹100 today will cost ₹106 next year.
  • If you keep ₹100 idle in cash or in a 3% savings bank account, your real purchasing power actually declines.
  • To protect capital, your long-term returns must exceed the rate of inflation.
Key Takeaway: Keeping all money in a low-interest checking account is not zero risk—it guarantees loss of real buying power!
3. Compound Interest & Rule of 72
Earning interest upon accumulated interest

Compound interest allows your money to grow exponentially because each period's earnings are reinvested into the principal:

A = P × (1 + r/n)(n × t)

The Rule of 72: Divide 72 by your annual interest rate to find out how many years it takes to double your capital.

Example: At an 8% annual return, your money doubles in approximately 72 / 8 = 9 years!
4. Credit Cards & Loan Traps
Good debt vs predatory short-term debt

Loans and credit cards can build creditworthiness when managed responsibly, but roll-over balances carry steep financial penalties:

  • Credit Card Interest: Unpaid balances often incur 36% to 42% APR. Paying only the "Minimum Amount Due" leads into a debt spiral.
  • Instant Loan Apps: Beware of predatory digital micro-lenders that charge exorbitant processing fees and compromise phone privacy.
  • Credit Score (CIBIL): Timely payments build a healthy 750+ score, which lowers interest rates on future education and home loans.
5. The Emergency Safety Net
Financial cushion for unforeseen crises

An Emergency Fund consists of 3 to 6 months of essential living expenses parked in liquid, easily accessible instruments (such as a High-Yield Savings Account or Liquid Mutual Fund).

  • Prevents you from taking high-interest personal loans during sudden laptop repairs, medical events, or job transitions.
  • Should never be invested in volatile assets like individual stocks or cryptocurrency.
6. Investment Risk-Return Spectrum
Higher return expectation mandates higher capital risk

No investment offers both zero risk and sky-high returns simultaneously:

Low Risk: Bank FD, Treasury Bills Medium Risk: Index Funds / Large Cap Equity High Risk: Small-Cap, Crypto, F&O
Golden Rule: Diversify across asset classes and avoid putting all capital into a single stock.
