About This Project

Academic methodology, technology stack, ethical standards, and research limitations.

Project Title & Academic Background

Title: “Financial Literacy Among College Students: A Data Science and Machine Learning Analysis”
Target Qualification: Bachelor of Science in Data Science (B.Sc Data Science)
Core Domain: Behavioral Analytics, Educational Data Mining & Supervised Machine Learning.

This project investigates undergraduate students' understanding of personal finance, banking technologies, credit systems, and investment risks. By gathering structured survey responses and utilizing objective scoring rubrics, the system pinpoints critical knowledge gaps and classifies students into actionable literacy tiers.

Data Science Methodology & Pipeline

Phase 1
Survey & Data Collection
Design of a privacy-preserving questionnaire evaluating 8 core knowledge domains without harvesting sensitive PII.
Phase 2
Data Cleaning & Scoring
Deduplication, missing-value handling, categorical standardization, and objective scoring calculation using Pandas.
Phase 3
Exploratory Data Analysis
Univariate and bivariate statistical analysis, correlation checks, and knowledge gap rankings.
Phase 4
Machine Learning & Deployment
Decision Tree classification (preventing data leakage), metrics validation, and deployment via Flask web framework.

Dataset Disclosure & Ethics

Synthetic Data Notice: For demonstration, testing, and viva defense, this project includes a realistically generated synthetic dataset (N=500 records) modeled on empirical student financial literacy studies. It accurately reflects observed college trends (e.g. high familiarity with mobile UPI payments alongside severe gaps in inflation, compound interest, and investment instruments).

Privacy Protection: The survey architecture strictly avoids collecting Personally Identifiable Information (PII). No student names, mobile numbers, Aadhaar/SSN IDs, bank account numbers, or login credentials are ever requested or stored.

Academic Limitations

  • Sample Representation: Survey responses collected from a single institution or demographic cohort may not generalize to the entire national student population.
  • Self-Reporting Bias: Surveyed students may occasionally overestimate their budgeting discipline or reported saving consistency.
  • Non-Causal Associations: High correlation between prior financial courses and test scores does not imply that curriculum attendance alone guarantees prudent real-world financial behavior.
  • Model Scope: The Decision Tree model serves solely as an educational classifier; it should not be utilized for commercial credit scoring or underwriting decisions.
